# Fondo – who else does this job, at what price, on whose money

**In plain English.** Fondo sells startups three things in one subscription: someone keeps their books, someone files their company tax return, and someone claims the research tax credit for them. We wrote a test for "does the same job", checked more than 70 companies against it on their own websites, and found 22 that pass – the biggest is Pilot, with more than USD 158 million raised. Fondo's prices sit in the middle of the pack. Nobody who does exactly this job has been sold at a published price, so there is no honest sale multiple to quote for it.

## 1. Answer up front

**Crowded.** 22 operators pass all three legs of the test below on their own websites – 13 material, 7 minor, 2 micro – and that is a floor: the long tail of certified public accounting firms with a startup practice was sampled, not counted. **Three matter most.** **Pilot** does the same three jobs for the same buyer, has raised more than USD 158 million (USD 1.2 billion valuation in 2021), and is the operator Fondo's own pricing page compares itself to. **Haven** is the closest look-alike – same three jobs, same buyer, Slack-based support, and its own blog ranks itself the top Fondo alternative. **Zeni** sells the same bundle with USD 47.5 million raised. **What differentiates Fondo on public evidence is narrow:** quarterly (USD 1,198) and annual (USD 950) bookkeeping close options, which none of the four like-for-like rivals publishes on its pricing page; a corporate tax package (USD 1,950 list) priced below Pilot (from USD 2,450) and Zeni (USD 2,499 plus USD 300 for Delaware); and Delaware franchise tax included. On monthly bookkeeping its USD 499 list price is mid-range (USD 200–549 across the like-for-like five). **Counter-reading:** the price edge is thin and partly promotional – Fondo's current offer (USD 399 a month, USD 1,450 tax) is a "limited time offer", and Pilot's page applies its own 20% promotion. No former direct competitor was found; the deals in the space are adjacent and mostly distressed. This note is a research artifact, not an investment recommendation.

## 2. How to read this note

### The test

A company counts as a competitor of Fondo if it does Fondo's job – in Fondo's words, "full ownership of your bookkeeping, corporate taxes, and R&D tax credits" (fondo.com, retrieved 2026-11-11) – as **its own service, for the customer**. Three observable legs, written on function, not on vocabulary:

```
  L1  KEEPS THE BOOKS        a recurring close (monthly, quarterly or annual) done FOR the
                             customer by the operator's people or agents – not software the
                             customer or its own accountant runs
  L2  FILES THE RETURN       prepares and files the customer's US federal corporate income
                             tax return (Form 1120, C corporation), sold by the operator as its
                             own service (performed in-house or by a firm it subcontracts)
  L3  FILES THE CREDIT       prepares and files the customer's research and development tax
                             credit claim (Form 6765), sold by the operator as its own service

  DIRECT          passes L1 + L2 + L3, each evidenced by a quote from its own site
  FORMER DIRECT   once passed all three; no longer trades independently
  INDIRECT        fails at least one leg but contests the same customer, price point or budget
  UNVERIFIED      own site not located, blocked, or entity not resolved – no ruling made
  EXCLUDED        fails the legs AND does not contest the same customer (listed in Method only)
```

Rulings that bend a leg, written down so a reader can repeat them:

- **Buyer is an axis, not a gate.** An operator selling all three legs to small businesses in general (Bookkeeper360) is direct; the buyer difference is named in its row.
- **Subcontracted delivery counts** for L2 and L3 if the operator sells the service on its own page. Fondo's own terms allow it to "perform any or all of its obligations hereunder through engagement of a third party accountant" (fondo.com/fondo-terms, Fondo Agreement dated 2024-11-22), so the same allowance is given to Puzzle, whose tax is prepared by "a vetted partner accounting firm".
- **A referral is not a sale.** Digits points customers to "one of our accounting Partners"; it fails L2 and L3.
- **A free calculator is not a service.** Acorn9 and Inkle offer research and development credit calculators but no credit-filing service on the pages read; they fail L3.
- **Geography falls out of L2.** An operator that files only Spanish, Australian or Emirati returns fails L2 and, not contesting a US Delaware corporation, is excluded.
- **Fondo through its own test:** passes all three (pricing page: "Federal Corporate Tax Filing (form 1120)", "R&D Tax Credit Study (Form 6765)", monthly / quarterly / annual close).

## 3. The market

A US startup incorporated as a Delaware C corporation owes, every year, a set of filings it rarely has staff for: monthly or periodic books (investors and the tax return both need them), a federal corporate return (Form 1120) plus state returns, a Delaware franchise tax report, and – if it spends on engineering – a research and development credit claim (Form 6765), which a young company can apply against payroll tax. Historically these were bought from a local CPA firm by the hour, or split across a bookkeeper, a tax preparer and a credit specialist who takes a share of the credit. The product category Fondo sits in bundles all three as a subscription delivered through software plus accountants. Fondo describes itself as an "all-in-one accounting platform for startups" (fondo.com/pricing) and, per TechCrunch (2024-11-22), had about 1,200 customers, USD 6 million ARR and was profitable at its seed round.

The same job is sold under at least four vocabularies – "startup accounting", "outsourced finance / back office", "AI-native accounting firm" and plain "CPA firm" – which is why the sweep below does not rely on Fondo's own words.

**Pilot** (pilot.com). Bookkeeping by a "US-based bookkeeper who handles, explains, and reviews your books"; C corporation tax "Must be purchased with Pilot Bookkeeping"; research and development credit at 20% of the credit. It is the scale player: Form D filings show USD 60.6 million sold in the first Series C tranche and USD 30.2 million in the extension by the filing date; TechCrunch put total funding above USD 158 million and the valuation at USD 1.2 billion (2021-03). Pilot also runs comparison pages against Fondo (pilot.com/pilot-vs-fondo) and about 60 other providers – it treats this as one market. **Counter-reading:** Pilot sells to small businesses broadly and has moved into software for accounting firms ("Meridian. The AI operating system for accounting firms" banner), so startup tax may be a smaller share of its attention than of Fondo's.

**Haven** (usehaven.com). Same three jobs, same buyer, same pitch ("Bookkeeping, Taxes, and R&D Credits – handled by a team that actually responds"), Slack-native support, and a credit fee charged only if the credit is received. Funding beyond the 2024 pre-seed (amount not disclosed) could not be tied to this company – see Funding.

**Zeni** (zeni.ai). Automation-led bookkeeping "backed by finance experts", tax from USD 2,499 a year, credit at 20%. Higher entry price than Fondo; Delaware franchise tax is a USD 300 add-on where Fondo includes it.

**Kruze Consulting, Burkland Associates, inDinero, Graphite Financial, Escalon, Aprio** are the traditional-firm end: all three legs, higher or unpublished prices, sold as engagements. Fondo's own pricing page names this group ("CPA/Accounting Firm") as its second comparison.

**Synthesis.** The deal market for this exact product is **not proven**: no company that does all three jobs has sold at a disclosed price, and the one direct competitor in a deal (Puzzle) is selling a different asset. The adjacent exits are small, undisclosed and often distressed – Bench after a shutdown, Finally out of an ABC, MainStreet after a down round. **Buyers active in adjacent deals** – the kinds of acquirer this evidence points to for the category, not a prediction for Fondo: back-office platforms selling to the same buyer (Gusto, Employer.com / Mainstreet), accounting-software groups (Xero; Money Forward, which led Fondo's seed), AI-native firm consolidators (Inkle, Accrual), and private-equity-backed accounting firms. **Supportable multiple range: none.** Only one multiple recomputes – 13.4× annualised revenue for Xero–Melio, a bill-pay software benchmark, not a product analog; the services-firm bound (Citrin Cooperman) cannot be computed from disclosed figures. A range "bounded by" these two would pair software with a CPA firm and would not be a range of direct analogs. **Kept apart, as a valuation event:** Fondo's own seed priced it at USD 66 million on USD 6 million ARR (TechCrunch, 2024-11-22) – 11.0×.
